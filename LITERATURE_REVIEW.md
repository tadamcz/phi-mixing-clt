Literature assessment — 5 September 2026

**Finding: the submission appears to address a genuine longstanding open problem. I found no earlier classical counterexample meeting all its hypotheses. Its novelty remains conditional on the mathematical correctness and faithful formalization of the submitted construction.** The strongest recent affirmative evidence of open status is a 2023 scholarly article explicitly restating the same conjecture. The search also covered later work, including a preprint revised in August 2026; none of the relevant results inspected resolves it.

The precise target is a strictly stationary, centered, real-valued process with

```math
\phi(n)\to0,\qquad \mathbb E X_0^2\lt \infty,\qquad
\sigma_n^2=\mathrm{Var}(S_n)\to\infty,
```

for which $`S_n/\sigma_n`$ fails to converge in distribution to $`N(0,1)`$. Here $`\phi`$ is the uniform conditional-probability coefficient, including the supremum over all positive-probability conditioning events. The normalization is the actual standard deviation of the partial sum. These details distinguish this problem from several already-solved problems with almost identical titles.

In this repository, that target is the theorem at [Challenge.lean:134](Challenge.lean#L134). The proposed counterexample is assembled at [Spec.lean:12865](submission/Spec.lean#L12865), and the disproof appears at [Spec.lean:12898](submission/Spec.lean#L12898).

Other apparent resolutions were checked. Merlevède–Peligrad (2000), “The functional central limit theorem under the strong mixing condition,” resolves a **different Bradley conjecture**, involving a quantitative α-mixing/quantile condition; it is not an Ibragimov–Iosifescu resolution. [Author-uploaded primary text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/38351350_The_functional_central_limit_theorem_under_the_strong_mixing_condition). A MathOverflow search snippet mentioning a published counterexample was attached to the **Cantelli conjecture**, in a different answer on the same page. [Page context](https://mathoverflow.net/questions/37151/what-are-the-big-problems-in-probability-theory). A recent paper titled “On Relative Stability for Strongly Mixing Sequences” also concerns α-mixing and infinite second moments. [Primary article](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-9321/5/4/33).

The contribution that appears potentially new is therefore the **combination** of genuine φ-mixing, finite second moments, divergent partial-sum variance, and failure of the standard-deviation-normalized CLT. Non-Gaussian behavior under weaker mixing, and the phenomenon of variance being sustained by increasingly rare events, have substantial precedent. The submission's bounded causal correction and rare-spike construction should be presented against that background. This search does not establish priority for every individual construction technique or lemma.

There are useful consistency consequences. By Peligrad's positive theorem, any valid counterexample here must have $`\liminf_n\sigma_n^2/n=0`$. Similarly, it cannot have a finite $`(2+\delta)`$-moment for any $`\delta\gt 0`$, given Ibragimov's stronger-moment CLT discussed in Berkes–Philipp's introduction. These are **deductions conditional on the counterexample being correct**, not variance asymptotics independently verified in this literature search. The proof's existing lower variance bound and total-variation approximation alone do not establish an upper variance bound.

A valid scalar CLT counterexample would also refute the corresponding Brownian weak invariance principle, by evaluating at time 1. It would additionally imply a negative answer to the general φ-mixing-implies-ρ*-mixing question, using the known implication to the CLT recorded in Bradley's survey. These are mathematical implications; the submission directly formalizes the scalar disproof.
